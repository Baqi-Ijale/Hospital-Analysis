Hospital Readmission & Patient Outcome Summary
The hospital dataset contains 1,000 patient records. The average length of stay is approximately 7.46 days, while the average treatment cost is approximately 330,278. The overall 30-day readmission rate is 21%.
The readmission analysis shows that October had the highest monthly readmission rate at approximately 26.74%. Among the departments, Cardiology had the highest readmission rate at 27.74%, with 38 patients being readmitted.
Age is also an important factor in the data. Patients aged 61 and above had the highest readmission rate at approximately 37.71%, which is considerably higher than the other age groups. This group may therefore benefit from closer follow-up after discharge.
The data also shows that patients with shorter hospital stays had higher readmission rates. Patients who stayed for 1–4 days had a readmission rate of approximately 28.62%. This shows an association between shorter stays and readmission in this dataset, although it does not prove that shorter stays directly cause readmissions.
In terms of patient outcomes, 69.8% of patients recovered, while 22.3% improved, 6.7% were referred, and 1.2% were deceased. Treatment cost did not show a simple relationship with better outcomes. The deceased group had the highest average treatment cost, while the recovered group had a lower average cost.
From the department analysis, Oncology and Surgery are departments that management may want to examine further because they combine relatively high treatment costs with notable proportions of patients who were deceased or referred. ICU had the highest average treatment cost, but its proportion of these outcomes was lower, so additional investigation would be needed before drawing conclusions.

Recommendations
1.	Monitor readmission rates regularly, especially in Cardiology.
2.	Provide closer post-discharge follow-up for patients aged 61+.
3.	Review patients with very short hospital stays to understand the reasons behind their higher readmission rate.
4.	Investigate the causes of higher treatment costs in departments such as ICU and Oncology.
5.	Continue monitoring patient outcomes and treatment costs to identify areas where hospital resources can be used more effectively.
6.	Use the Power BI dashboard regularly so management can filter results by department and month and monitor changes over time.
